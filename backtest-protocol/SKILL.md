# Backtest protocol

Stages, never skipped: backtest → walk-forward → paper → small live.

**Use the built-in tools first.** `bt_list_strategies` shows the strategies, their parameters and the rule format; `bt_run` backtests one (fees, slippage, Hyperliquid funding, next-bar fills, buy-and-hold over the same window, saved to `backtests/<id>.json`); `bt_walk_forward` does the in-sample/out-of-sample split, k folds and a small parameter grid; `bt_to_paper` starts the same strategy forward in paper. Express custom entry/exit conditions as `rules` (data, no code). Write your own backtest code only for what they can't express (several assets, portfolio rebalancing, order-book data), and then follow every step below by hand.

1. **Pre-register before any result.** Write `strategy.md` in the workspace first: the hypothesis in one sentence (why this edge should exist and who pays for it), universe, timeframe, entry, exit, sizing, costs, the pass/fail test (e.g. out-of-sample return above buy-and-hold *and* DSR ≥ 0.95 *(default)*) and the parameter values you will try. Fix them before looking at results. If you peek or change anything afterwards, write down what and why; a changed hypothesis is a new trial, not a fix.
2. **Data.** `bt_run` fetches and pages public history itself (Hyperliquid perps, Binance and other spot exchanges), keeps closed bars only and reports the source, gaps and any shortfall (Hyperliquid serves only the latest 5,000 candles; Kraken about 720): quote those notes. For your own code, fetch the same public endpoints from a script and save CSVs with source and fetch time; check gaps, duplicates and UTC timestamps; drop the last candle if it is still open.
3. **No lookahead.** A decision at bar t uses data up to t's close and fills at t+1's open or later (the built-in engine does this, and is tested for it). No indicators computed on the full series and then sliced; no universe chosen with today's knowledge (survivorship). Add a test: changing future bars must not change past signals.
4. **Costs.** Fees per fill (check the venue's current schedule; roughly Hyperliquid taker 0.045%, Binance spot 0.1%, DEX pools 0.05–0.3% plus gas). Slippage of at least half the spread plus impact for the size, more for small caps. Perp funding per hour held. Report results with and without costs; if it only works without them, it doesn't work.
5. **Metrics.** Return vs buy-and-hold, max drawdown and the Monte Carlo p95 drawdown (`risk.monteCarloDrawdown`: the same returns reshuffled; plan for the p95, not the one path you saw), Sharpe, Sortino and Calmar (state the annualization), ulcer index, CVaR 95% per bar, win rate with average win and loss, trade count, time in market, turnover. Under ~30 trades the result is an anecdote: say so. Metric definitions follow quantstats (Apache-2.0).
6. **Multiple testing.** Every variant you try is a trial, kept or not: `bt_run` and `bt_walk_forward` count every parameter set ever run on that market and bar size (deleting a result doesn't lower it) and report `deflated`: PSR (probability the true Sharpe is above 0, given length, skew and kurtosis) and DSR (the same against the best Sharpe luck would produce over that many trials; Bailey & López de Prado 2012, 2014). Report DSR with its trial count. Below 0.95 *(Orbie's default bar)*, say the result is within what luck explains; the Strategies view warns on it before "Make it an agent".
7. **Overfitting.** Few parameters. Sweep them and look for a plateau, not a spike (`bt_walk_forward` lists the top in-sample sets per fold). Walk-forward: fit on one window, test on the next unseen one, roll forward, and report only the stitched out-of-sample results. **Freeze the split by date before the first run** (in `strategy.md`) and never move it after seeing results. Keep a final holdout untouched until the end. When the walk-forward searched a grid, report its `overfit` block: PBO (how often the in-sample best set ranks below the median set out of sample, by CSCV; above 0.5 *(Orbie's default bar)* the search mostly fits noise, and the Strategies view warns before "Make it an agent") and the CPCV path-Sharpe range (wide or negative paths mean the result depends on the period).
8. **Robustness.** Bull, bear and sideways periods; similar assets; signals one bar late; costs doubled.
9. **Paper.** `bt_to_paper` with the chosen `bt_run` id runs the same strategy forward on live prices in its own paper account (`bt_paper_runs` to check it, `bt_paper_stop` to end it). For your own code, feed its signals to `paper_order` (a `client_order_id` and a short reason on every order). Run it for days to weeks and compare paper fills with what the backtest expected. New paper accounts use the book fill model on Hyperliquid coins (market orders walk the live book; limit orders wait in the queue and pay the maker fee), so a maker strategy (`entry_limit_bps`) that filled on candles may fill less in paper: that gap is the point of the test. Label it "paper". The paper run is the forward test of the pre-registered hypothesis: judge it by the test written in step 1.
10. **Live.** As an agent with small per-trade and daily limits on a schedule. Write kill criteria first (e.g. drawdown over 1.5x the backtest's worst) and review against paper after a set number of trades.

**Stop on a failed test.** If the walk-forward, the DSR bar or the forward (paper) test fails the pre-registered test, report it and stop: don't search for a threshold, window, filter or market that passes, and don't re-run the same idea with tweaks until it does (that is how lucky backtests get promoted). Add it to `negative-results.md` in the workspace (hypothesis, what was tried, trial count, why it failed) so it isn't retried. Starting over needs a genuinely new hypothesis, written down first, and it counts as more trials.

Report: the pre-registered hypothesis and test, assumptions, the out-of-sample result next to buy-and-hold over the same window, its DSR and trial count (the chat shows `bt_run`'s equity curve, drawdown and trades), a metrics table with costs, what wasn't modelled, and what would break it. It is a simulation on past data: never present in-sample results, or any backtest, as expected returns.
